## Stitch and embroidery planning
Tatami fill covers large areas. Satin stitches are appropriate for narrow borders and lettering. Stitch totals vary with logo size, density, underlay, fabric stretch, and digitizer choices; an automatic estimate is a planning starting point, not a production file. Use a cutaway backing for stretchy knits and stabilize caps with backing selected for the machine and hoop.

## Garment selection and production
Heavyweight cotton fleece and French terry are common choices for hoodies and crewnecks. Combed cotton jersey suits soft T-shirts; pique is a structured option for polos. Structured six-panel caps need cap frames and cap-driver-compatible equipment. Test each garment, thread, needle, and backing combination on a sample before a full production run.

## Quotation and lead times
A quote depends on garment choice, order quantity, placements, stitch count, packaging, finishing, and turnaround. Volume tiers can lower per-piece garment cost. Express or rush scheduling should be confirmed against the shop's live capacity. Digitizing can be offered as a separate line item or waived for qualifying quantities. Quotes should state validity dates and assumptions.

## Quality control and delivery
Inspect thread tension, registration, trims, backing, and garment condition on a production sample and at defined intervals. Record defects and corrective actions in the order's quality log. Confirm approved artwork, garment sizes, quantities, labels, packaging, and destination before dispatch.

## Safety and payment
StitchAI demo checkout records a simulated payment transaction only. It does not charge a card or initiate a real Stripe or Razorpay payment. Production orders should not be marked paid based on a simulation in a live business workflow. Never send card numbers, passwords, API keys, or other payment secrets to the assistant.
